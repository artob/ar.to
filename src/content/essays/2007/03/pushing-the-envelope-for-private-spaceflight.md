A couple hours ago, aerospace startup SpaceX
[succeeded](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.spacex.com/updates.php)
in launching a two-stage [Falcon
1](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.spacex.com/falcon1.php)
rocket to
[sub-orbital](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital_spaceflight)
space, attaining an altitude of 300 kilometers before the second stage [shut
down
prematurely](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.spaceflightnow.com/falcon/f2/status.html).
Anomalies aside, this is a pretty significant step in the [privatization of
spaceflight](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_spaceflight)
— for perspective, consider that the [International Space
Station](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station)
orbits the Earth at approximately 360 km.

[![Falcon 1 Demo-2
T-00:00:02](./20070321-spacex-1.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.spacex.com/video_gallery.php)

Blast-off from the [Omelek
Island](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omelek_Island)
launch pad at [Kwajalein
Atoll](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwajalein_Atoll)
in the Pacific.

This
[second](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_1#First_flight_failure)
Falcon test flight, dubbed the Demo-2, didn’t yet reach its intended circular
orbit of 685 km, to where it was to deliver a 50 kg experimental
[NASA](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.nasa.gov/) payload. It
did, however, achieve the primary mission objective of validating the rocket’s
structural design and avionics, and set a new private spaceflight altitude
record to boot.

Space buffs will recall that it was just three years ago, in 2004, that the
[CSXT](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Space_eXploration_Team)
team launched the first private rocket to reach the final frontier (gaining a
verified altitude of 115 km), and [Scaled
Composites](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.scaled.com/) won the
[X
Prize](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansari_X_Prize)
with their
[SpaceShipOne](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceShipOne)
craft that made the first privately funded human spaceflight (to 112 km).

So, tech
[hectomillionaire](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millionaire)
and space entrepreneur [Elon
Musk’s](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk)
bold venture is starting to pay off — good thing, too, as he reportedly has
invested over \$100 million of his personal wealth to bootstrap the company.

The [Falcon
1](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_1)
is billed as the world’s first privately funded and developed liquid-fueled
orbital launch vehicle, and is currently priced at \$7 million a pop —
significantly cheaper than any previous orbital delivery solution. It’s a
two-stage kerosene—liquid-oxygen design capable of delivering a
[LEO](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit)
payload of up to 670 kg. The rocket is built to be simple, reliable and
dirt-cheap. As Elon
[says](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.spacex.com/media.php?page=44):

> *“The Merlin is much more analogous to a truck engine than a sports car
> engine, which is how all other engines are designed. Instead of designing it
> to the bleeding edge of performance and drawing out every last ounce of
> thrust, we designed Merlin to be easy to build, easy to fix and robust. It can
> take a beating and still keep going.”*

Elon has stated that his dream is to revolutionize access to space by ultimately
improving cost and reliability by a factor of 10 over existing solutions.

There’s also a
[feature](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.nasaspaceflight.com/content/?cid=5052)
and a lively post-mortem
[discussion](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://forum.nasaspaceflight.com/forums/thread-view.asp?tid=7169)

detailing the pre-launch drama of the last couple days (including the aborted
ignition at T-0 yesterday), and what exactly went wrong in the second stage of
the flight today.

Anyway, it looks like 2007 is going to be a major milestone in private
spaceflight. SpaceX has two more launches [lined
up](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.spacex.com/launch_manifest.php)
this year, with commercial payloads no less, and I’d be surprised if they
couldn’t attain their objective of being the first in the emerging private
aerospace industry to achieve low-Earth-orbit payload insertion.

Congratulations are in order to Elon & the team. They are directly partaking in
shaping the very future of humanity by getting us [back on
track](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.amazon.com/High-Frontier-Human-Colonies-Apogee/dp/189652267X)
to becoming a true [spacefaring
civilization](https://web.archive.org/web/20070321/http://www.amazon.com/Entering-Space-Creating-Spacefaring-Civilization/dp/1585420360).
